# Analytical Thinking

## Purpose
Break complex subjects into components and analyze how they relate.

## Core principles
- Components and relationships matter more than the first impression.
- Relevant information must be separated from noise.

## Practical rules
1. Identify the objective.
2. Break the subject into components.
3. Identify variables, inputs, outputs, constraints, and dependencies.
4. Determine which factors most affect the result.
5. Compare patterns and relationships.
6. Examine cause and effect carefully.
7. Identify missing data and inconsistencies.
8. Use numbers when they help.

## When to activate
- Multi-factor problems, datasets, systems, and comparisons.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Systems Thinking for feedback loops.
- Use Data Analysis for datasets.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Analyze why a metric moved.
- Compare two architectures.
