# Systems Thinking

## Purpose
Examine the whole system and how a local change can affect other parts.

## Core principles
- Parts interact.
- Second-order effects matter.
- Fixing the visible problem may not fix the system.

## Practical rules
1. Identify the system objective and components.
2. Map interactions, inputs, outputs, dependencies, and feedback loops.
3. Find bottlenecks and single points of failure.
4. Distinguish direct from indirect effects.
5. Consider incentives.
6. Look for trade-offs where improving one part hurts another.
7. Propose interventions at leverage points.

## When to activate
- Complex projects, organizations, technical systems, workflows, strategy.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Strategic Thinking for long-horizon position.
- Use Process Design to change an operational flow.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Trace why a local patch created a new outage.
