# Lateral Thinking

## Purpose
Reframe the problem when the obvious method is blocked, expensive, or failing.

## Core principles
- The stated problem may be the wrong problem.
- Reverse a constraint when useful.

## Practical rules
1. Identify the conventional approach and its assumptions.
2. Reframe from another perspective.
3. Look outside the obvious category.
4. Test unconventional ideas against practical constraints.
5. Return to the original objective and check that the new path still solves it.

## When to activate
- Stuck problems, expensive obvious solutions, repeated failure of the same method.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Creative Thinking to expand options.
- Use Problem Solving to execute the chosen path.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Get a result without the blocked tool.
