# Decision Making

## Purpose
Evaluate choices systematically instead of defaulting to preference.

## Core principles
- Criteria before preference.
- Trade-offs must be visible.
- Reversible decisions need less analysis than irreversible ones.

## Practical rules
1. Identify the decision and the objective.
2. List options and evaluation criteria.
3. Weigh benefits, costs, risks, opportunity costs, and long-term effects.
4. Separate facts from assumptions.
5. Consider action and inaction.
6. Watch for sunk-cost and confirmation bias.
7. Do not choose for the user when the choice is values-based. Show trade-offs.

## When to activate
- Choosing between options, setting priorities, accept/reject decisions.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Prioritization when ranking many tasks.
- Use Risk Assessment for downside.
- Use Scenario Analysis when several futures remain live.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Choose a tool stack.
- Decide whether to accept an offer.
