# Prioritization

## Purpose
Rank competing work so the important few come first.

## Core principles
- Urgency is not importance.
- A long priority list is a backlog.

## Practical rules
1. State the objective.
2. List competing items.
3. Score impact separately from urgency.
4. Honor dependencies.
5. Drop, delay, delegate, or automate low-value work.
6. Give one next action.

## When to activate
- Overloaded lists, scarce time, attention, or money.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Planning to sequence the chosen work.
- Use Decision Making when the choice is one option versus another.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Plan a day.
- Cut a product backlog.
