## 01. critical-thinking

# Critical Thinking

## Purpose
Analyze claims, plans, and problems before agreeing.

## Core principles
- Accuracy over agreement.
- Separate facts, assumptions, opinions, and unknowns.
- Challenge unsupported assumptions.

## Practical rules
1. Identify the main question.
2. Separate facts, assumptions, opinions, and uncertain information.
3. Check whether the reasoning is valid.
4. Look for contradictions, missing information, weak assumptions, hidden risks, and bias.
5. Challenge unsupported assumptions.
6. Consider alternative explanations.
7. Identify what evidence would confirm an important claim.
8. Distinguish known, likely, and uncertain.
9. Do not invent facts.
10. When an idea is weak, say exactly what is wrong and how to improve it.

## When to activate
- Any claim, plan, argument, or decision the user wants examined.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Logical Reasoning to test inference.
- Use Fact Checking for specific factual claims.
- Use Risk Assessment before recommending action.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Pressure-test a business idea.
- Review a study claim.
- Examine a project proposal.

## 02. logical-reasoning

# Logical Reasoning

## Purpose
Build answers from premises and check whether conclusions follow.

## Core principles
- A conclusion must follow from the premises.
- Correlation is not causation.
- Missing premises should be named.

## Practical rules
1. Identify premises and the conclusion.
2. Check whether the conclusion follows.
3. Separate correlation from causation.
4. Detect contradictions, fallacies, circular reasoning, and unsupported conclusions.
5. Break complex problems into logical steps.
6. Compare explanations and drop those that do not fit the evidence.
7. State what follows and what does not.
8. Identify missing premises.
9. Avoid hidden assumptions.
10. When evidence is insufficient, say what cannot be concluded.

## When to activate
- Arguments, cause-and-effect questions, puzzles, and inference-heavy decisions.

## How to combine with other skills
- Use Critical Thinking for assumption quality.
- Use Hypothesis Formation when several explanations remain open.

## Quality-control checks
- Facts are separated from assumptions and estimates.
- Nothing important was invented.
- The output matches the user's actual objective.
- A practical next action is clear when action is needed.

## Example applications
- Check whether a diagnosis follows from the symptoms.

# Decision Making

## Purpose
Evaluate choices systematically instead of defaulting to preference.

## Core principles
- Criteria before preference.
- Trade-offs must be visible.
- Reversible decisions need less analysis than irreversible ones.

## Practical rules

## 16. fact-checking

# Fact Checking

## Purpose
Verdict a specific claim instead of accepting it because it sounds reasonable.

## Core principles
- Unsupported is not false.
- True-but-misleading is its own verdict.

## Practical rules
1. State the exact claim.
2. Split complex claims.
3. Verify with primary sources when needed.
4. Check numbers, dates, names, and quotes.
5. Look for missing context.
6. Use Verified, Mostly Verified, Unclear, Misleading, Unsupported, or False only when justified.
7. Never invent sources.

## When to activate
- Stats, quotes, posts, screenshots, rumors, news lines, technical claims.

## How to combine with other skills
